Privacy policy of CineWise: 3D Movies from Your NAS

Last updated: 20 November 2026

This policy explains what data CineWise: 3D Movies from Your NAS ("the app") collects on a Meta Quest headset, how the app uses that data and how you can ask us to delete it. The developer is dav3studios. The publisher is David Lee ("we", "us").

What data the app collects

The app collects the user data in this table and processes it on the headset.

This data stays on the headset, with two exceptions that you start yourself. The app sends your sign-in details to the servers that you add, to sign in. When you sign in to Plex, the app sends plex.tv the name and version of the app and a random number that it makes for this installation. The app does not send this data to us or to an advertising or analytics company. We receive a problem report only if you email it to us.

The app requests no data from your Meta account: no user ID and no age group. "What Meta does" below says more.

It has no account of its own, no chat, no advertising, no analytics and no in-app purchases. Our own code connects only to the servers that you add, and to plex.tv when you sign in to Plex. When you select Find my NAS, the app asks the devices of your home network whether they share folders. This question does not leave your home network.

The app plays video files and photos from the places that you add: a NAS (a network storage device) or a computer on your network through the SMB file sharing protocol, a WebDAV server such as Nextcloud, a Plex Media Server, the shared folders of the headset such as Movies and Download, a folder on the headset or a USB drive. "What the app keeps on the headset" says what each item is and why the app uses it. It also says how long each one stays and how to delete it. "How to ask us to delete your data" says how to ask us.

What the app keeps on the headset

Your sign-in details

  • What it is: The server addresses, user names and passwords that you add stay on this headset, in encrypted storage. The storage is the app's own private storage, encrypted with a key that the Android Keystore of the headset holds. For a folder on the headset or a USB drive, the app keeps only the permission that you gave in the system folder picker.
  • Why the app uses it: The app uses them only to connect to those servers. When it signs in, it sends the user name and password to the server, as the SMB or WebDAV protocol asks.
  • Where it goes: They are never sent to us or to anyone else. The app sends them only to the server at the address that you added.
  • How long it is kept: Until you delete it, or until you uninstall the app.
  • How to delete it: Edit > Forget this source deletes them. Uninstalling the app deletes them too.

Plex

  • What it is: If you add a Plex server, the app asks plex.tv, the website of Plex Inc., for a sign-in code, and you type the code on the Plex website. The app uses the key that Plex then gives it once, to get the list of your Plex servers, and forgets it. It keeps only the key and the address of the server that you pick, in encrypted storage on the headset.
  • Where it goes: plex.tv gets the request for the code, the name and version of the app, and a random number that CineWise makes for this installation, because Plex asks every app for one. The privacy policy of Plex says what Plex does with it. The app sends the server key only to your Plex server. Movies come straight from your server, not through plex.tv.
  • How long it is kept: Until you delete it, or until you uninstall the app.
  • How to delete it: Edit > Forget this source deletes the key. You can also remove CineWise from the devices of your Plex account on the Plex website.

The list of your files

  • What it is: The app reads the names and sizes of the files in the folders that you open. It plays the files from your servers, from the headset or from a USB drive. If you allow access to photos and videos, the app also reads the names and sizes of the videos and photos in the shared folders of the headset, such as Movies and Download. Android asks you before the app gets this access. The app only reads these files, to show and play them. It never changes, moves or sends them.
  • Why the app uses it: To show you the list of movies and photos, and to play the one that you pick. The app also reads the subtitle files in the folder of a movie, to offer them in the subtitle picker.
  • How long it is kept: It keeps this list in the headset's memory only. When the app closes, the list is gone.
  • Where it goes: Nowhere. The video data comes from your server to the headset over your network.
  • How to delete it: Nothing to delete: the list is gone when the app closes. To take back the access to photos and videos, switch it off in the app's permissions in the headset settings.

What the app remembers about files

  • What it is: For each file that you play, the app remembers the 3D format that you chose, the place where you stopped, your sound and subtitle choice and the subtitle delay. It stores this on the headset under a code made from the file's place, not under the file name. It keeps the 400 newest files. Separately, for Continue watching, the app keeps the names and places of the last 20 videos that you played, in encrypted storage on the headset. For files on the headset or a USB drive, the app keeps small pictures from your videos and photos in its cache on the headset, to show in the list. Your settings, such as the volume and the subtitle size, also stay on the headset.
  • Why the app uses it: To show each file the way that you chose last time, and to continue a movie from where you stopped.
  • Where it goes: Nowhere. It stays in the app's own storage on the headset.
  • How long it is kept: Until you delete it, or until you uninstall the app.
  • How to delete it: About > Delete what CineWise remembers about files deletes it. The same button deletes the Continue watching list and the pictures. Clear history on the Continue watching page deletes only that list. Uninstalling the app deletes it too.

Problem reports

  • What it is: If the app stops because of a fault, it saves a short problem report on the headset. The report holds the app version, the headset model and system version, and the place of the fault in the program. It holds no file names, no addresses and no account data. The app never sends it.
  • Why the app uses it: So that you can send it to us, if you choose, and we can fix the fault.
  • Where it goes: Nowhere, unless you send it. On the next start, the app offers "Save the report to Downloads" and "Delete the report". If you save it, you can email the file to us yourself.
  • How long it is kept: On the headset, until you save it or delete it. A report that you email to us is kept until the fault is fixed, and for at most 12 months. We use it only to fix the fault.
  • How to delete it: "Delete the report" on the home panel. Uninstalling the app deletes it too. To delete a report that you emailed to us, see "How to ask us to delete your data".

Movies that you save to the headset

  • What it is: A movie that you save to the headset stays in the app's own storage on the headset. It is a copy of the file on your server. While a movie saves, the app keeps its name and place in encrypted storage on the headset, so that the download can go on after the app closes. The app also saves when you close it or the headset sleeps. A notification shows while it saves.
  • Why the app uses it: To play the movie with no network, for example on a journey.
  • How long it is kept: Until you delete it, or until you uninstall the app. The name and place of a movie that saves are kept until the download ends, or until you cancel it or remove it from the list.
  • How to delete it: Delete removes a saved movie. Uninstalling the app deletes everything that the app keeps.

The app makes no backup of any of this. The headset's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for the app.

Your hands

If you use your hands instead of controllers, the headset tells the app where your hands are and when you pinch or tap your thumb. The app uses this only to point, select and control the movie. It does not keep or send it. The app's Android settings ask for the hand tracking permission for this.

Network

The app's Android settings ask for the internet permission and the network state permission. The app needs them to reach the servers that you add. Our own code connects only to the servers that you add, and to plex.tv when you sign in to Plex. It never connects to us, to an advertising or analytics service, or to any other server. When you select Find my NAS, the app asks the devices of your home network whether they share folders. This question does not leave your home network. If you add an address outside your home network, the app connects to that address, as you asked.

A WebDAV address that starts with https uses encryption. An address that starts with http does not, so the password goes over the network as plain text. The app warns you before it saves such an address.

What Meta does

Your Meta account

When the store version starts, Meta's software on the headset checks that your account owns the app. This is the entitlement check of the Meta Platform SDK, Meta's software library inside the app. The app gets only a yes or no answer. It does not read your user ID, your name or your age group. The app does not store or send the answer.

The app requests no data from your Meta account in Meta's Data Use Checkup: no user ID, no age group, no profile, no friends and no purchases.

Meta's software libraries in the app, the Spatial SDK and the Platform SDK, work with the Meta system software on the headset. That system software may contact Meta. Meta's own privacy policy covers that part.

The headset, the Meta account and purchases are covered by Meta's own privacy policy: https://www.meta.com/legal/privacy-policy/ and https://www.meta.com/legal/quest/privacy-policy/.

Camera, microphone and location

The app never asks for the headset cameras, the microphone or your location. The app shows a virtual cinema, not your room.

How to ask us to delete your data

You can delete everything that the app keeps on the headset yourself: see "What the app keeps on the headset" above. Uninstalling the app also deletes it.

To ask us to delete your data, email us at lee_hantae@msn.com with the subject "Delete my data" and the name of the app. Anyone can ask, in any country, and it is free. We reply within 30 days. We delete any data that we hold about you, for example a problem report that you emailed to us, and our reply confirms the deletion. Our reply also tells you how to delete the data that the app keeps on the headset.

Meta holds the data of your Meta account. To ask Meta to delete it, use your Meta account settings or Meta's privacy policy.

Children

The app is for ages 13 and up. It is not made for children under 13. It collects the same data for every user: the table at the top lists it.
